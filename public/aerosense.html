AeroSense AQ-1 · Research

Mapping Dhaka's air,
from above.

AeroSense is FX-13 Valour's drone-based air-quality project. We fly a sensor-carrying drone over some of Dhaka's busiest places and log what is in the air every five seconds: fine particles, carbon monoxide, nitrogen dioxide, temperature, humidity and pressure.

      How we measured

      About the data.

      What the drone carries

      • A particle sensor for PM1.0, PM2.5 and PM10: dust and smoke small enough to breathe deep into the lungs.
      • Gas sensors for carbon monoxide (CO) and nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), mostly from traffic exhaust.
      • Temperature, humidity and air-pressure sensors.
      • One reading every 5 seconds during each flight.

      The limits we compare with

      • WHO 2021 guideline (24-hour): PM2.5 15 µg/m³, PM10 45 µg/m³.
      • Bangladesh national standard (24-hour): PM2.5 65 µg/m³, PM10 150 µg/m³.
      • Each flight lasted 5 to 11 minutes, so these comparisons are indicative snapshots, not official 24-hour averages.

      Notes on the numbers

      • Particle readings come from the team's repaired flight logs.
      • CO and NO₂ are converted from the raw sensor signal with an indicative calibration, so read them as rough estimates.
      • Bijoy Sarani's summary was calculated from its own flight log, which is shorter than the others (65 readings).
